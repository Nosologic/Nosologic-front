## Summary
At high altitude, lower ambient oxygen (hypobaric hypoxia) elicits physiological responses governed by HIF (hypoxia-inducible factor) pathways. These responses alter innate and adaptive immunity, vascular biology, and metabolism. When populations adapted to high altitude move to near-sea-level environments (normoxia), immunologic set points may change. In our framework this “ancestral condition” (hypoxia) can contribute, over longer timescales, to population-level differences in tuberculosis risk upon descent, motivating the evseq hypothesis linking hypoxia to tuberculosis.
